Privacy policy.

This policy explains what personal data Resource Ramp Private Limited collects through this website and the incubation programme, why it collects it, who it is shared with, and the rights people have over it.

Effective 11 September 2026. Applies to resourceramp.co and to the Resource Ramp incubation programme.

This policy is written to comply with the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (the DPDP Act) and the rules made under it, and with the Information Technology Act, 2000 and the Information Technology (Reasonable Security Practices and Procedures and Sensitive Personal Data or Information) Rules, 2011 (the SPDI Rules), to the extent those continue to apply.

2. What this policy covers

It covers personal data collected from:

  • founders and teams who apply to, join or complete the incubation programme, including their co-founders and early employees where the applicant supplies that information;
  • mentors, angel investors, corporate and institutional partners, sponsors and their representatives;
  • people who attend demo days, workshops, Founder Fridays and events hosted at the hub, including Local Startup Meet, Mumbai 2026;
  • interns, candidates and vendors dealing with the Company; and
  • visitors to this website.

Where an event or programme is run jointly with another organisation, that organisation may be a separate data fiduciary for the data it collects, and its own notice applies alongside this one. The Company says so at the point of collection.

4. Why the data is used, and on what legal basis

Under the DPDP Act, personal data is processed either with the person's consent or for a legitimate use recognised by the Act. The Company relies on the following:

  • Assessing an application. Application data is used to decide whether a startup is offered a place in a cohort. Basis: the applicant's consent, given when the application is submitted, and the fact that the applicant has voluntarily provided the data for that purpose.
  • Running the programme. Data about selected startups is used to allocate seats, match mentors, run one-on-ones, workshops and demo days, track progress, and perform the incubation agreement. Basis: performance of the incubation agreement and the startup's consent.
  • Introducing startups to mentors, investors and partners. With the founders' consent, application and progress information is shared with programme mentors and the angel investor panel during selection and during the programme, and with corporate partners when a startup is shortlisted against a partner's problem statement. Basis: consent, which can be withdrawn.
  • Events. Registration data is used to manage attendance, security and follow-up. Photographs and recordings are used to document the event and, where the attendee has been told at registration, in the Company's own communications. Basis: consent given at registration.
  • Reporting to partners, sponsors and public bodies. Aggregated, de-identified programme data is used in partner reports and in applications for recognition or support from bodies such as the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade or state startup initiatives. Individual data is shared only where the founder has agreed or the law requires it. Basis: consent, or legal obligation.
  • Legal and statutory compliance. Records are kept and disclosed where the Companies Act, 2013, tax law, or a lawful order of a court or authority requires. Basis: legal obligation.
  • Keeping the website and the hub secure. Server logs and visitor records are used to detect misuse and keep people safe on the premises. Basis: legitimate use for security.

The Company does not sell personal data, and does not use it for advertising, profiling or automated decisions that have a legal effect on a person. Selection decisions are made by people.

5. Who the data is shared with

  • Resource Workspaces. The hub sits inside Resource Workspaces' premises. Names, seat allocations and access details of cohort members are shared with Resource Workspaces so that people can use the space.
  • Mentors and the investor panel. As described in section 4, with consent. Mentors and investors are asked to keep the information confidential and to use it only for the programme.
  • Corporate and institutional partners. Only when a startup is shortlisted against that partner's problem statement, and only the information needed for the partner to evaluate the introduction. Any commercial relationship that follows is contracted directly between the partner and the startup.
  • Vetted service partners. Startups that choose to use a partner firm for incorporation, compliance, accounting or marketing deal with that firm directly and under its own terms. The Company passes on only the introduction.
  • Service providers. The website is hosted on Vercel. Application forms are delivered by FormSubmit, a form-to-email service, which transmits the form contents to the Company's mailbox and does not use them for its own purposes. Email runs on Zoho Mail. These providers process data on the Company's instructions and may store it outside India; the Company takes reasonable steps to ensure they protect it.
  • Authorities and advisers. Where the law requires, or to the Company's own auditors, lawyers and accountants under a duty of confidence.

6. How long data is kept

  • Unsuccessful applications: up to 24 months from the decision, so that founders who reapply for a later cohort do not have to start from scratch, unless the applicant asks for earlier deletion.
  • Selected startups: for the duration of the programme and for eight years after it ends, which is the period the Companies Act, 2013 and tax law require the Company to keep books and records that reference the relationship.
  • Mentor, investor and partner records: for as long as the relationship is active and for three years after the last engagement.
  • Event registrations: 24 months after the event.
  • Server logs: 90 days, unless a security incident requires longer.

When the retention period ends, the data is deleted or de-identified.

7. Security

The Company keeps personal data in access-controlled mailboxes and cloud storage, restricts access to the programme team and the people named in section 5, uses encrypted connections for the website and for email, and reviews who has access when people join or leave. No system is perfectly secure. If a breach of personal data occurs that is likely to affect a person's rights, the Company will inform the affected people and the Data Protection Board of India as the DPDP Act requires.

8. Cookies and tracking

This website sets no cookies of its own and runs no analytics, advertising or social media tracking scripts. The web fonts are loaded from Google Fonts, which means Google receives the visitor's IP address when the font files are requested. The Company's hosting provider may set technically necessary cookies for security. If this changes, this section will be updated and a consent notice added before any non-essential cookie is set.

9. Rights of data principals

Under the DPDP Act a person whose data the Company holds may:

  • ask for a summary of the personal data held about them and how it has been processed and shared;
  • ask for inaccurate or incomplete data to be corrected or updated;
  • ask for data to be erased once the purpose it was collected for has been served, unless the law requires it to be kept;
  • withdraw consent at any time, with effect from that point onward. Withdrawing consent to share an application with mentors and investors may mean the application cannot be assessed further;
  • nominate another person to exercise these rights in the event of death or incapacity; and
  • raise a grievance with the Company and, if it is not resolved, with the Data Protection Board of India.

Requests should be sent to the grievance officer named below. The Company responds within 30 days, and may ask for proof of identity before acting on a request.

10. Children

The programme and this website are for adults. The Company does not knowingly collect personal data from anyone under 18. If a parent or guardian believes a minor's data has been collected, they should write to the grievance officer and it will be deleted.
